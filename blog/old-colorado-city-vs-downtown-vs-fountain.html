Old Colorado City vs. Downtown vs. Fountain: Colorado Springs Guide

By Brandon Armstrong, REALTOR®, The Front Range Team, Real Broker, LLC · October 26, 2026

Old Colorado City, Downtown Colorado Springs, and Fountain each give you a different version of life in the Pikes Peak region. Old Colorado City is a historic west-side neighborhood, Downtown is the original core of Colorado Springs, and Fountain is a separate city next to Fort Carson. The right fit comes down to your commute, the kind of home you want, and what you want close by.

Old Colorado City: El Paso County's first permanent settlement

Old Colorado City started as its own town. According to the Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum, Colorado City was the first permanent settlement in El Paso County, founded in 1859 as a trade supply post along Fountain Creek. Its voters approved joining Colorado Springs, and the annexation took effect on June 10, 1917.

That history is still easy to see. History Colorado notes that the Old Colorado City Historic Commercial District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on November 2, 1982. It runs along the north side of Colorado Avenue from 24th Street, with examples of Queen Anne, Italianate, and Romanesque Revival architecture among its 27 properties. The City of Colorado Springs adds that the commercial district was almost torn down in the 1970s before local activists saved it.

If you're looking here, I'd pay attention to:

  • Age of the home. If you're looking at an older house, a thorough inspection matters, and so does budgeting for updates.
  • Street parking and lot size. These vary a lot from block to block, so tour in person.
  • Proximity to the Colorado Avenue shops, if being near that district is part of the appeal for you.

Downtown Colorado Springs: the city's original core

Downtown is where Colorado Springs began. The Downtown Partnership says Acacia Park, at the center of Downtown, is the city's first park, donated by city founder Gen. William Jackson Palmer in 1871. Downtown also has America the Beautiful Park in the southwest corner, with access to the Midland and Pikes Peak Greenway trails, and Alamo Square Park, which surrounds the Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum.

Southwest Downtown is also home to the U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum. Its fact sheet says it opened to the public on July 30, 2020, in a 60,000-square-foot building.

Things to think about Downtown:

  • Home type. If you're shopping condos, lofts, or townhomes, read the HOA documents closely.
  • Parking and storage. Ask whether a unit includes a garage space or a storage unit.
  • Event activity. Parks and venues host events throughout the year, so visit on a weekend evening, not just a quiet weekday morning.

Fountain: its own city next to Fort Carson

Fountain isn't a Colorado Springs neighborhood. It's a separate city with its own city hall on Main Street. The City of Fountain says the town was founded in 1859, the same year as Colorado City, and incorporated as a town in 1903. The Fountain Trading Company building, established in 1898, still stands at 117 Main Street. Fort Carson was founded in 1942 and has been Fountain's neighbor ever since.

For military buyers, that location is a big part of the draw. Fort Carson's relocation team notes that the post's Carson Connector shuttle runs in partnership with City of Fountain Transit.

When I help buyers compare Fountain, we look at:

  • Commute. Drive the route to your gate or your job at rush hour before you commit.
  • Taxes, fees, and services. Fountain is a separate city, so compare its property taxes, HOA or special district fees, and utility providers with Colorado Springs addresses.
  • Different parts of town. Tour a few areas of Fountain rather than judging the whole city from one street.

I've spent 10+ years in business and closed 200+ transactions in Colorado Springs and the surrounding areas, and I'm always glad to show buyers these three areas side by side. See more on my neighborhoods page, or read my Fort Carson PCS buying guide if you're moving on orders.

Frequently asked questions

Is Old Colorado City part of Colorado Springs?

Yes. Colorado City was founded in 1859 and was annexed by Colorado Springs on June 10, 1917, according to the Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum. Today it's a neighborhood on the city's west side.

Is Fountain part of Colorado Springs?

No. Fountain is a separate city. The City of Fountain says it was founded in 1859 and incorporated as a town in 1903, and Fort Carson became its neighbor in 1942.

What is there to do in Downtown Colorado Springs?

Downtown is home to Acacia Park, the city's first park, plus America the Beautiful Park, the Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum, and the U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum, which opened in July 2020.

How do I choose between these three areas?

Start with your daily routine: your commute, how much yard and space you want, and whether you'd rather be near a historic district or a newer part of town. Then tour each area on a weekday and a weekend before you write an offer.

Bottom Line

Old Colorado City and Downtown put you inside Colorado Springs near its oldest landmarks, while Fountain is its own city next to Fort Carson. Tour all three at different times of day before you decide.
